\section{MAP-XAI Packet Construction and Accounting}

The endpoint computes the attribution change relative to its last successfully
delivered explanation and clips it as
\begin{equation}
\overline{\Delta\vec\phi}_{i,t}=
\frac{\Delta\vec\phi_{i,t}}
{\max\{1,\|\Delta\vec\phi_{i,t}\|_2/C_i\}}.
\end{equation}
It then applies a Gaussian mechanism,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\Delta\vec\phi}_{i,t}=
\overline{\Delta\vec\phi}_{i,t}+\vec n_{i,t},\qquad
\vec n_{i,t}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{i,t}^2\Delta_i^2 I_d),
\end{equation}
before Top-$k$ selection and quantization. Because these two compression steps
operate on the perturbed vector, they are post-processing. For $d$ attribution
coordinates, header size $S_0$, and $q$ magnitude bits, the packet length is
\begin{equation}
S_i(m)=S_0+k\bigl(\lceil\log_2d\rceil+q+1\bigr),
\end{equation}
where one additional bit stores the sign.

At RDP order $\alpha$, an admitted Gaussian release costs
$\rho_{i,t}(\alpha)=\alpha/(2\sigma_{i,t}^2)$. Composition and conversion at
the end of the protected horizon give
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i(\delta_i)=\min_{\alpha}
\left[\sum_t a_{i,t}\rho_{i,t}(\alpha)
+\frac{\log(1/\delta_i)}{\alpha-1}\right].
\end{equation}
The accountant rejects a candidate whose projected expenditure exceeds the
declared horizon budget. A rejected candidate and an explicit abstention add
no content release. An admitted packet is charged when exposed outside the
trusted endpoint, even if its later radio delivery fails.

\section{PRISM-XAI Scheduling Procedure}

For each slot, MAP-XAI first enumerates packet modes that fit the remaining
privacy budget. PRISM-XAI filters these modes using resource-block and deadline
constraints, scores the survivors from public state, and selects at most one
mode per endpoint. The multiple-choice knapsack core has complexity
$O(NMB)$ for $N$ endpoints, $M$ candidate modes, and integer radio budget $B$.

\section{Formal Results and Proofs}

\begin{proposition}[Sensitivity of the clipped household contribution]
Let
$g(\vec x)=\Delta\vec\phi(\vec x)/
\max\{1,\|\Delta\vec\phi(\vec x)\|_2/C_i\}$ and represent an absent
household by the zero contribution. Under add/remove contribution adjacency,
$g$ has $\ell_2$ sensitivity at most $C_i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The clipping map projects every contribution into the closed $\ell_2$ ball
$\mathcal B_2(C_i)=\{v:\|v\|_2\leq C_i\}$. Under the stated add/remove
adjacency, one dataset contains endpoint $i$'s clipped contribution $g(\vec x)$
and its neighbor contains the zero contribution. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\|g(\vec x)-g(\varnothing)\|_2
=\|g(\vec x)-0\|_2\leq C_i.
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over adjacent inputs gives sensitivity at most $C_i$.
This is not a replacement-adjacency bound: if one household record could be
replaced by an arbitrary second record, the triangle inequality would give
the looser bound $2C_i$. The theorem below consequently uses the add/remove
model stated in the main paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Packet-content privacy over the protected horizon]
Fix endpoint $i$ and assume add/remove contribution adjacency, an $\ell_2$
sensitivity bound $\Delta_i$ for every conditionally generated clipped delta,
and Gaussian noise with standard deviation $\sigma_{i,t}\Delta_i$ at each
exposed release. If the accountant admits only sequences satisfying
\begin{equation}
\min_{\alpha>1}\left\{\sum_t a_{i,t}
\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma_{i,t}^{2}}+
\frac{\log(1/\delta_i)}{\alpha-1}\right\}\leq\epsilon_i,
\end{equation}
then the released explanation-packet contents for endpoint $i$ satisfy
$(\epsilon_i,\delta_i)$-differential privacy over the protected horizon.
The statement remains valid when future noise levels and release decisions
adapt to public state and previously released private outputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition on an arbitrary realized public-state history and on all previously
released private outputs. By the sensitivity assumption, the next clipped
delta differs by at most $\Delta_i$ between adjacent inputs. The Gaussian
mechanism with noise standard deviation $\sigma_{i,t}\Delta_i$ is therefore
$(\alpha,\rho_{i,t}(\alpha))$-RDP with
$\rho_{i,t}(\alpha)=\alpha/(2\sigma_{i,t}^{2})$. Adaptive composition of RDP
mechanisms adds their conditional RDP parameters, so the complete sequence of
exposed noisy deltas is
$(\alpha,\sum_ta_{i,t}\rho_{i,t}(\alpha))$-RDP for every tracked order
$\alpha>1$. Converting RDP to approximate DP gives
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i(\delta_i)=\min_{\alpha>1}\left\{
\sum_ta_{i,t}\rho_{i,t}(\alpha)+
\frac{\log(1/\delta_i)}{\alpha-1}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The admission rule ensures this value never exceeds $\epsilon_i$. Finally,
packet construction after perturbation---including Top-$k$ selection,
quantization, encoding, and reconstruction---is post-processing and cannot
increase privacy loss. Thus the released packet contents satisfy the claimed
horizon guarantee. The proof concerns packet contents; any additional
data-dependent observable must be included in the mechanism before the same
claim can be extended to it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Privacy preservation of packet formation and public-state
scheduling]
Assume a noisy attribution delta has already been released by a mechanism with
RDP parameter $\rho(\alpha)$. Any randomized Top-$k$ selection, quantization,
encoding, reconstruction, or scheduling transformation whose additional
inputs are public state and previously released private outputs remains
$(\alpha,\rho(\alpha))$-RDP with respect to the protected contribution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $P$ and $Q$ be the distributions of the noisy delta under adjacent inputs,
and let $K$ denote the Markov kernel implementing all subsequent packet and
scheduling transformations. Because $K$ receives no additional unprotected
function of the current private contribution, the output distributions are
$PK$ and $QK$. R\'enyi divergence obeys the data-processing inequality,
\begin{equation}
D_\alpha(PK\|QK)\leq D_\alpha(P\|Q)\leq\rho(\alpha).
\end{equation}
Thus the transformations cannot increase the RDP parameter. Randomized
transformations are included because a Markov kernel may contain independent
randomness. Adaptive use of earlier private outputs is also included by
conditioning on the realized history at each step and then applying adaptive
composition. The condition on additional inputs is essential: a scheduler
that directly reads an unprivatized current attribution residual is not
covered by this post-processing argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Zero additional content expenditure under abstention]
If no new protected packet is exposed in slot $t$, then slot $t$ contributes
zero to the packet-content RDP sum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $a_{i,t}=0$, the slot emits no new packet content. Its contribution in the
composition expression is
$a_{i,t}\rho_{i,t}(\alpha)=0$ for every $\alpha$. Updating public controller
state or retaining a previously released explanation is either independent of
the current private delta or post-processing of already released information,
so it creates no additional packet-content mechanism. This does not assert
that silence itself is private if silence is an observable, data-dependent
event; that question belongs to a transcript-level model.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Complexity of the slot-level allocation]
For $N$ endpoints, at most $M$ feasible modes per endpoint (including the
zero-transmission choice), and integer resource budget $B$, the
multiple-choice knapsack core is solvable in $O(NMB)$ time and $O(B)$ memory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $V_i(b)$ be the maximum accumulated utility after processing endpoints
$1,\ldots,i$ with at most $b$ resource units. For endpoint $i$, mode $m$ has
integer cost $c_{i,m}$ and utility $u_{i,m}$. The recurrence is
\begin{equation}
V_i(b)=\max_{m:\,c_{i,m}\leq b}
\{V_{i-1}(b-c_{i,m})+u_{i,m}\}.
\end{equation}
There are $N(B+1)$ states and at most $M$ transitions per state, yielding
$O(NMB)$ time. Only the preceding row is required to compute the next row, so
two length-$(B+1)$ arrays---or one array with suitable buffering---give
$O(B)$ memory. Feasibility filtering and score construction occur before this
core and are not included in the bound, exactly as stated in the main paper.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete Result-Figure Atlas}

This section reproduces every vector result figure generated by the three
validated experimental matrices. For each configuration, it includes the
combined-dataset, OPSD, and UCI versions of the representative regime trace,
fairness--reliability analysis, privacy--fidelity analysis, traffic--fidelity
Pareto analysis, and scheduler ablation results. The corresponding PNG files
are duplicate raster exports and remain in the reproducibility package; the
vector PDF versions are used here for print quality. Thus the atlas contains
$3$ configurations $\times$ $3$ scopes $\times$ $5$ figure families $=45$
generated result figures.

\subsection{How the five figure families should be read}

\paragraph{Representative regime trace.}
These panels show the temporal mechanism behind the aggregate metrics:
prediction or explanation-quality variation, cumulative traffic, remaining
privacy allowance, and transmission activity over the evaluation horizon.
They reveal whether a policy communicates steadily, exhausts its allowance
early, or uses sparse opportunities. A single trace is illustrative and is
not used for statistical inference; the paired aggregate tables provide that
evidence.

\paragraph{Fairness--reliability plane.}
These plots place explanation fidelity or service reliability against Jain
delivery fairness. A point near the right boundary serves endpoints evenly,
but fairness alone does not establish adequate coverage or explanation
quality. The relevant question is therefore whether traffic savings move the
proposed method into a worse fairness--reliability region, not simply which
point is farthest right.

\paragraph{Privacy--fidelity plane.}
These plots compare maximum composed privacy expenditure with agreement-based
explanation fidelity. They test whether a larger privacy allowance visibly
improves the measured proxy. Flat or noisy trends indicate that clipping,
Gaussian perturbation, compression, or the dataset may dominate the available
budget increase; they do not establish semantic usefulness by themselves.

\paragraph{Traffic--fidelity Pareto plane.}
These plots expose the communication cost of each operating point. Policies
with much larger traffic can also occupy different coverage and recall
regions, so geometric dominance in this plane is not sufficient for the
paper's matched-service claim. Only the paired PRISM-XAI versus MAP-XAI-only
contrast is interpreted as an incremental scheduler comparison.

\paragraph{Ablation panels.}
The ablations remove one design element at a time and report the resulting
metric changes. They identify whether savings arise from packet compression,
adaptive mode choice, the release cap, or the candidate score. Because several
metrics and ablations are tested, the numerical tables and BH-adjusted tests
remain the inferential reference.

\section{Interpretation of the Extended Evidence}

The complete results support three focused conclusions. First, public-score
PRISM-XAI reduces traffic relative to the MAP-XAI-only component while meeting
the declared matched-service and recorded-privacy rule. Second, the stronger
reduction is specific to the public-score formulation: the residual-score
configuration yields only a small saving, and the stricter $M=4$ cap alone
does not produce a detectable traffic difference. Third, explanation fidelity
is dataset dependent. OPSD exceeds the random Top-$K$ reference, whereas UCI
does not. The communication result therefore does not imply universal
explanation usefulness.

All 720 runs in each matrix pass the recorded validation checks, with no
duplicate experimental keys and no observed accountant overspend. The study
remains a scheduler-level evaluation with synthetic zones and a reproducible
radio trace. A field deployment should predefine required service and fidelity
levels, evaluate real interference and retransmissions, and add a
transcript-level mechanism when communication metadata is considered private.
